I've started four products. Each move came from something I noticed while the previous one wasn't working. This is how they connect.

## InterviewPass: customers expected a bigger product

InterviewPass was an asynchronous interview experience. A candidate records their answers on their own time, and a recruiter reviews them later. It did that one job well.

The trouble was what people expected from it. They wanted an entire hiring platform, not one step of the process. We tried to close the gap by integrating with ATS providers, and we couldn't make that scale.

If customers keep describing a bigger product than the one you built, that's a signal.

## Gumstack: it worked, but only when traffic existed

Gumstack let a shopper on a retailer's website place an audio or video call to someone in the store, and shop on the call.

It worked. We watched real deals close over video calls, including a ₹4 lakh sale for Urban Ladder. Brands like Urban Ladder, Furlenco, Koskii and PN Rao used it.

But calls were a function of traffic. Roughly a tenth of visitors made a call, so a store with less online traffic got fewer calls. To get value from Gumstack, customers first had to spend money building traffic. For retailers whose main channel wasn't online, that never made sense, and they churned.

## The thing we noticed

During this time we saw something that, at first, looked small. After a video call ended, the rest of the conversation didn't stay on the site. The follow-up questions, the details, and the closing of the deal all happened on WhatsApp.

The call opened the conversation, but WhatsApp was where it carried on. That raised an obvious question: why not help businesses talk to their customers there directly?

## Peach

That is how Peach started. It began as a shared inbox, then grew into broadcasts, then automations, and then AI agents.

## What the four have in common

Each time, the signal was in how customers behaved. With InterviewPass, it was what they expected the product to be. With Gumstack, it was where the conversation moved after our product ended. Watching that told us more than asking anyone what they wanted.
